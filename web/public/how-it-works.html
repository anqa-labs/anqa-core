A perpetual futures exchange with a dark order book: a real on-chain CLOB where nobody — not other traders, not bots, not the sequencer-watchers — can read resting orders or positions. Only the trades themselves print. Built on Solana inside a MagicBlock ephemeral rollup, with Percolator as the risk kernel.

Why dark

A lit order book leaks everything: your resting orders advertise your intent, your position invites hunting, and every taker's flow is copyable in real time. Serious size does not want to trade in a glass room. anqa keeps the mechanics of a central limit order book — price-time priority, partial fills, real counterparties — while making the book itself unreadable.

Architecture

The venue lives on two layers at once, and the split is the design: custody never leaves base chain; execution never leaves the rollup.

Ephemeral rollup — MagicBlock

Dark order book Portfolios (margin accounts) Risk group — Percolator Oracle relay (Pyth) Public tape

The trading floor. ~50ms slots, no gas races, accounts readable only by policy — and physically unreadable on a TEE validator.

Delegation is MagicBlock's primitive: an account's ownership is temporarily handed to the rollup, which executes transactions against it at rollup speed and later commits final state back to base. anqa delegates the book, portfolios, risk state, oracle relay and tape. The custody vault is deliberately never delegated — a rollup bug can corrupt a price, never a balance.

Privacy is native, not bolted on. The book account carries an access-control list naming exactly one reader: the matching engine. On the public devnet rollup that is on-chain policy; on MagicBlock's TEE validator the RPC itself enforces it, so the order book bytes never leave the enclave. No zero-knowledge circuits, no mixers — just an account nobody may read.

Life of a trade

Deposit — base chain

USDC moves into the custody vault; a monotonic ledger records it. This is the only moment tokens move.

Start session — one wallet signature

Your margin account (portfolio) is delegated into the rollup, and in the same transaction a browser-held session key is granted trading rights. It can trade — and only trade. It cannot touch funds, and you can revoke it from base at any time. This is the last popup you see.

Claim — inside the rollup

The rollup credits your portfolio from the base ledger. Your collateral is now live margin.

Order — signed silently

Pay × leverage defines your size. The order crosses the dark book under price-time priority; it can slice across many counterparties. The program checks the oracle band first — no fill may stray from the mark, because a perp fill mints value at its price.

Match ≠ settle — the dark decoupling

On a lit book the taker names the makers it crossed. Here it cannot see them — so matched fills queue on the book instead of settling inline. Matching decides who trades at what price; nothing else.

Settle — the engine's crank

A permissionless keeper drives settle_fill: each queued fill is executed through the Percolator risk kernel, which mints the long/short pair or refuses on margin. The engine adds liveness, never authority — it can only execute what the book already matched, at the price it matched, between the parties it named.

Print — the tape

The one public artifact: price, size, time. No maker, no taker, no side.

Hold — marked to market

The keeper cranks the mark from Pyth every ~2s and refreshes open positions, so PnL, margin and liquidation risk track the live price. Take-profit / stop-loss triggers sit off-book inside your portfolio — reduce-only by construction, invisible to the market until they fire.

Close & withdraw — back across the boundary

Closing is a reduce-only cross of the book (it can never flip you). Withdrawal is a three-leg handshake: base reserves a receipt, the rollup's kernel authorizes it against your equity, base pays out. The rollup approves; only base moves money.

The risk kernel

Every fill settles through Percolator, a formally-specified margin engine that anqa embeds as its solvency authority. It nets positions (one account, one position per market), computes equity and margin requirements, realizes losses eagerly and treats unrealized profit as junior to them, and refuses any trade a portfolio cannot carry. anqa's wrapper adds what the kernel deliberately leaves out: the oracle execution band, per-lot price scaling, and reserved margin for resting orders — so you cannot paper the book with orders you could never honour.

The venue's marks come from Pyth. Inside the rollup, Pyth's accounts are clone-read and relayed by the keeper, then accepted through five defences: source authenticity, staleness, confidence, cross-source deviation, and a move-band circuit breaker that freezes liquidations rather than mark on a manipulated print. Funding accrues against an EMA — a wick cannot drain a side.

Who runs it

The keeper — liveness, not authority

Permissionless loops anyone could run: relay the oracle, crank the mark and funding, settle queued fills, mark positions to market, sweep expired state, re-quote depth. Losing the keeper degrades the venue; it cannot steal from it.

The maker — depth on demand

A resident market maker quotes a ladder both sides of the mark so there is always someone to trade against. Every long you open is matched, dollar for dollar, by resting shorts — one counterparty or many, sliced by price-time priority.

Honest devnet caveats

  • This deployment runs on Solana devnet with play-money USDC (there's a faucet — help yourself).
  • On the public devnet rollup, book privacy is on-chain policy but not RPC-enforced; full enforcement is the TEE validator's job. Dark by design, TEE-ready.
  • Three markets run today — BTC, SOL and ETH perps — each a fully isolated venue: own book, vault, collateral and risk group.
